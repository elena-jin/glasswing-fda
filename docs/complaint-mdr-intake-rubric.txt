Connected-device complaint intake: high-recall rubric
For David's classifier · September 26, 2026
Decision: Send anything that could allege a deficiency in a marketed medical device, its regulated software/app, accessories, labeling, packaging or clinically relevant connection to a human Quality reviewer. Do not let this model close a complaint, decide MDR reportability, or make a legal determination. A zero-false-negative guarantee cannot be made by a classifier: use human review, sampling of negatives, drift checks and source-channel reconciliation.
Regulatory line
* Complaint is an intake concept, not an injury threshold. FDA's complaint inspection guidance describes a post-distribution oral, written or electronic communication alleging a deficiency in identity, quality, durability, reliability, safety, effectiveness or performance. Current law is the QMSR, effective February 2, 2026: 21 CFR 820.3, 820.10 and 820.35 incorporate ISO 13485:2016, including clause 8.2.2 for complaint handling. Cite old 820.198 only when discussing pre-transition enforcement, not as the live operative section.
* Complaint ≠ adverse event ≠ reportable MDR. A possible malfunction with no injury still goes to Quality. Under 21 CFR 803.50, a manufacturer generally reports within 30 calendar days of awareness of information from any source reasonably suggesting its marketed device may have caused or contributed to death or serious injury, or malfunctioned and would likely cause or contribute to death or serious injury if recurrent. Human regulatory staff determine reportability; do not wait for proven causation or perfect records before escalating.
* Serious injury under 803.3 means life-threatening injury/illness, permanent impairment/damage, or medical or surgical intervention needed to prevent permanent impairment/damage. "Caused or contributed" includes possible device failure, malfunction, design, manufacturing, labeling or user error. Five-work-day reporting is a distinct case under 803.53 when an MDR event requires remedial action to prevent unreasonable risk of substantial public-health harm, or FDA requests it.
Classifier rules
1. Capture every channel: support tickets and calls, field/service notes, app-store and ecommerce reviews, social posts, clinician/distributor reports, regulators, caregiver/third-hand reports, returned devices and trend data. Capture source text, date, product and version if known, reporter's account, clinical outcome, device role and missing facts; do not demand firsthand reporting, proof, a lot number or exact causation for routing. FDA cited missed ecommerce and foreign-regulator complaints and omitted user-error/component-wear feedback in its Contec letter.
2. Quality review if alleged failure or possible risk: false/stale/missing sensor readings or reports; loss, delay or corruption of sync or patient data; pairing and connectivity; missed, late, false, inaudible or confusing alarms; wrong dose/settings, missed therapy, action prompts or remote-control commands; crashes, battery/power, update/regression, login barrier to needed clinical function; usability/user-error patterns, misleading displays, instructions/labeling; degraded components; suspected tampering, unauthorized changes or vulnerable software with potential safety/effectiveness impact. The app may be the device or a component/accessory; determine its intended use rather than treating every screen as either regulated or unregulated.
3. Mixed or ambiguous => Quality review. "The graph looks wrong," "app never updates," "hard to see alerts," "I couldn't tell what my reading was" can affect use; do not downgrade merely because the message says UI, bug, known issue, user error, no harm, or "just feedback." A post-distribution allegation is enough to screen, while a bare request for a new theme, font or optional convenience feature without a failure or plausible clinical impact can remain product feedback. If intended use is unclear, route for review.
4. Priority subflags, not terminal labels: `possible_death_or_serious_injury`, `possible_recurrent_harmful_malfunction`, `clinical_risk_without_known_harm`, `cybersecurity_safety`, `needs_device_scope_check`, `insufficient_information`. Any of the first two triggers prompt regulatory/MDR assessment by trained staff. A security flaw without an exploited incident is not automatically an adverse event or MDR, but possible device control/data-integrity impact belongs in safety/risk review; see FDA cybersecurity.
5. Only clear negatives: preference or enhancement with no alleged current deficiency and no plausible clinical consequence ("Could the home screen have a dark purple option?"); generic praise, shipping questions unrelated to device integrity, billing, or spam. Route any uncertainty to review. Maintain separate product-idea tags on complaints rather than forcing a single mutually exclusive class.
QMS intake and adverse-event workflow
* Use a documented Quality intake procedure and formally assigned reviewers; log the raw report and channel, preserve evidence/version/timestamps, avoid PHI in training exports, deduplicate without dropping original reports, and document triage, investigation and rationale. Under 820.35(a), records for possible specification failures include review/evaluation/investigation; a similar prior investigation requires documented justification for no new one. For reportable, investigated or investigation-required complaints, record device name, receipt date, UDI/other identifier, complainant contact, nature/details, correction/action and reply.
* Investigate cause and risk with clinical, software, logs, version/build, device history, labeling and known recurrence. Track trends and feed risk management/CAPA and postmarket surveillance; do not mark "not reportable" as "not a complaint" or use missing version/lot as a reason to close. In its Medtronic letter, FDA cited inadequate CareLink software-error investigation where software versions were not sought, sensor complaints closed despite missing lot information, repeated retainer-ring complaints tied to an obsolete investigation, and missed injury/malfunction MDRs.
* Human review assesses 803.3/803.50 facts promptly: death; life-threatening condition; irreversible harm or intervention to prevent it; device contribution even through user error; and whether a malfunction would likely produce death/serious injury on recurrence. Do not mark every symptom as an MDR: an adverse experience is a safety signal for review; the legal MDR test is narrower. Preserve unknown clinical details as unknown; seek follow-up through approved procedures without holding the triage queue.
* Check other obligations separately when relevant: corrections/removals, cybersecurity, field actions, design/risk files, labeling, and regional vigilance. Off-label use is not a blanket exclusion: if a device may contribute to serious injury, or the report alleges device deficiency, route for review, note use context and let trained staff decide. A pure request for an unapproved feature may be an idea, not necessarily a complaint.
Examples and implementation
* "The app crashes when I try to read the morning glucose result" → Quality; possible clinical risk; seek whether care was delayed. "The green is ugly" → product feedback, if no readability/clinical context. "The tiny green number made me mistake 60 for 160" → Quality, urgent MDR assessment if harm/intervention.
* "MyAir hasn't synced for days" → Quality review if therapy data/monitoring is alleged unavailable or unreliable; do not assume clinical harm. "I'd like a different chart color" → product feedback unless readability affects clinical use.
* "My daughter says the caregiver app didn't alert her during my episode" → Quality and potential MDR review, even though third-hand. "The pump gave a dose I didn't program; my glucose fell" → urgent Quality/MDR review; FDA investigated a comparable insulin-pump/cybersecurity allegation in the Medtronic letter.
* "Camera won't rotate during gait test" → Quality review of intended clinical function, not automatic MDR; FDA cited an app orientation bug and risk/evaluation gaps in the SeniorLife letter. "Results are overwritten" → Quality and potential clinical risk. A patient's "cybersecurity vulnerability may alter readings" → Quality/security review; MDR only after human assessment of event criteria.
Suggested classifier prompt: "You are an intake screener, not a regulatory decision-maker. Extract the reporter's alleged facts without assuming they are true. If a released medical device, its software/app, accessory, labeling, packaging or clinically relevant data path may have failed or posed a safety/effectiveness problem, output `complaint_review_candidate=true`. If death, serious injury or potentially dangerous recurrent malfunction is possible, set the corresponding priority subflag. Unclear device scope, missing information, user error, no known injury, or third-hand reporting never justify a negative. Output `product_feedback_only=true` only when clearly a preference with no current failure or plausible clinical consequence. Supply short quoted evidence spans, uncertainty and follow-up questions. Never decide MDR reportability or tell a reporter their event is not a complaint."
Validation: Hold out real and synthetic edge cases separately, measure sensitivity and negative predictive value for Quality routing by source/product/clinical scenario, audit all false negatives and a stratified sample of negatives, and update labels with Quality adjudication. No model threshold guarantees zero misses; when uncertain, abstain to review.
